Youth employability services help teens and young adults get ready for work, land a job, and keep it. They cover career counseling, resume help, interview practice, soft skills, and paid work experience. Most are free for eligible people ages 14 to 24.

Getting hired is only half the challenge. Keeping the job matters just as much. Early exits often have little to do with skill. They usually come from a missed bus, a scheduling clash, or a late first paycheck. Strong youth employability services plan for both halves: getting the offer and surviving the first ninety days.

## What Are Youth Employability Services?

Youth employability services are programs that build the skills, documents, and support a young person needs to work. They serve people roughly ages 14 to 24. The goal is readiness, not just placement.

Employability and employment are different. Employment means holding a job. Employability means having what it takes to get one and keep it. That includes communication, reliability, basic money skills, and a resume that tells your story well.

**Most youth employability services include:**

- Career assessment and counseling
- Resume and application help
- Interview coaching
- Soft skills and workplace training
- Paid work experience or internships
- Support with transport, ID documents, and interview clothing

## Types of Youth Employability Services in the US

The right program depends on your age, school status, income, and barriers. The table compares the main options. Ages and rules vary by state.

| **Program** | **Typical ages** | **Best for** | **Cost** |
|---|---|---|---|
| WIOA Youth | 14-24 | Low-income youth with barriers, in or out of school | Free if eligible |
| Summer or year-round youth employment | Often 14-24 | A first paid job | Free; you get paid |
| Job Corps | 16-24 | Free residential career training | Free if eligible |
| YouthBuild | 16-24 | Out-of-school youth seeking a diploma plus trade skills | Free if eligible |
| Registered apprenticeship | 16+ | Paid, on-the-job training in a trade | You earn wages |
| Pre-employment transition services | Often 14-21 | Students with disabilities | Free through state agencies |

### WIOA Youth Program

The Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act funds a national network of youth employability services. Local workforce boards run it. Federal rules require at least 75 percent of local youth funds to go to out-of-school youth. At least 20 percent must fund work experience.

Source: [dol.gov](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/youth/wioa-formula), [clasp.org](https://www.clasp.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/WIOA-Title-I-Youth.pdf)

### Summer and Year-Round Youth Employment Programs

Many cities and counties run paid placements, often for ages 14 to 24. Youth work at local businesses, nonprofits, or city agencies for a set number of weeks. Some programs run year-round. They are often the easiest first step for someone with no work history.

### Job Corps and YouthBuild

Job Corps is a free residential program for ages 16 to 24. Students live on campus and train in a career field. YouthBuild serves out-of-school youth ages 16 to 24. Participants earn a diploma or equivalent while learning construction and other trades.

Source: [jobcorps.gov](https://www.jobcorps.gov/i-am-a/student), [dol.gov](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/youth/youthbuild)

### Apprenticeships

Registered apprenticeships pay you while you learn. Applicants must be at least 16 and meet the sponsor's requirements. Competition can be strong. Many youth employability services help with applications and aptitude tests.

Source: [dol.gov](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/training/youth)

### Programs for Youth with Disabilities

State vocational rehabilitation agencies offer pre-employment transition services to students with disabilities. These often include job shadowing, work readiness training, self-advocacy lessons, and paid internships. Age ranges differ by state, so confirm locally. Families can also explore our [autism and special needs support services](/services/autism-special-needs).

Source: [transitionta.org](https://transitionta.org/topics/pre-ets/)

### Reentry Programs

The Department of Labor funds Reentry Employment Opportunities programs for justice-involved young people. They focus on job training and support after release. Ask your local American Job Center what is available near you.

## Who Qualifies for Youth Employability Services?

Eligibility depends on age, school status, income, and barriers. Most publicly funded youth employability services serve ages 14 to 24 with at least one barrier to work. Local rules differ.

**Two categories matter most:**

- **In-school youth:** usually ages 14 to 21, enrolled in school, low income, with at least one barrier.
- **Out-of-school youth:** usually ages 16 to 24 and not attending school. Income rules are often less strict.

**Common barriers include:**

- School dropout
- Foster care
- Homelessness
- Pregnancy or parenting
- Justice involvement
- Disability
- English language learning
- Reading or math skills below grade level

Males ages 18 to 25 usually must register with Selective Service to join federally funded job training. Check sss.gov for the current process. Bring a photo ID, Social Security card, proof of address, and income documents.

## What to Expect: From First Meeting to First 90 Days

Most youth employability services follow one path: assessment, barrier removal, skills training, job search, and follow-up. You can enter at any step. Intake can take a few weeks because of eligibility paperwork.

1. **Assessment:** A counselor reviews your skills, interests, schedule limits, and goals.
1. **Barrier removal:** Staff solves practical problems before applications go out.
1. **Resume and applications:** You build materials that present your history honestly, including gaps.
1. **Interview coaching:** You practice the questions you dread, with direct feedback.
1. **Active job search:** Counselors keep you accountable so the search doesn't quietly stop.
1. **Follow-up:** Support continues through onboarding and your first paycheck cycle.

### Why Barrier Removal Comes First

Skipping this step is the most common reason a promising search stalls. A young person without ID cannot finish a hiring form. Someone without bus fare cannot reach an interview. Good youth employability services fix these problems early. Typical fixes include:

- Replacing lost ID documents
- Bus passes and route planning
- Interview clothing
- Childcare arrangements
- Email setup and computer access

Some young people also need [mental health counseling](/services/mental-health) alongside job help. Stress and instability make a job search harder.

## Core Skills Taught in Youth Employability Services

Youth employability services teach far more than resume writing. Programs vary, but training usually falls into four areas: job search, soft skills, money management, and digital skills. Each area targets a common reason young workers struggle to get hired or stay employed. Providers teach them through workshops, one-on-one coaching, and real work experience.

### Job Search and Application Skills

Nearly every application is online. Counselors show you how to search job boards, set up profiles on sites like Indeed and LinkedIn, and read a posting to spot what an employer actually wants. You learn to build a resume that fits the job, not a generic one you reuse everywhere.

Applying is only step one. Many young applicants never hear back, simply because they apply once and stop. Programs teach you to track every application, follow up within a week, and keep applying while you wait.

### Soft Skills

Employers value reliability and communication in entry-level roles more than almost anything else. A manager will often forgive a slow learner, but rarely forgives someone who shows up late or ignores instructions. Programs teach punctuality, showing up in the right uniform, and following basic workplace rules.

Communication is the other half. You practice asking for clarification instead of guessing, giving a manager a heads-up when you're running late, and taking feedback without treating it as personal criticism. Group activities and role-play exercises help build teamwork, since many young workers have never had to coordinate with coworkers on a shared task before. These habits protect a job long after training ends, and employers consistently name them as the top reason a young hire succeeds or fails.

### Financial Literacy for First-Time Earners

A first paycheck surprises many young workers. Net pay is lower than gross pay after taxes and withholding, and it often arrives later than expected, sometimes two weeks after the first shift. Without this context, a new worker can assume something went wrong.

Workshops walk through reading a pay stub, understanding tax withholding, and opening a checking account without hidden fees. You also learn basic budgeting, such as splitting a paycheck between needs, savings, and spending money. Some programs go further and cover building credit responsibly, avoiding predatory loans, and setting a small savings goal tied to a real purchase. Getting this right early makes it far less likely a young worker falls into debt problems later.

### Digital Skills

Employers assume you can use email, join video interviews, and clock in online. Many young people are comfortable with social media but struggle with these workplace basics, since the two skill sets are different.

Programs typically cover setting up a professional email address, writing clear work emails, and using video call platforms like Zoom or Teams for interviews. You also learn to use scheduling apps, submit timesheets, complete online training modules, and handle basic spreadsheets or point-of-sale systems, depending on the job.

## How to Choose the Right Program

Match the program to your situation:

- **Ages 16 to 17, in school:** Summer or year-round paid programs.
- **Ages 18 to 24, no diploma:** YouthBuild, or WIOA Youth with education support.
- **Want a skilled trade:** Apprenticeship or Job Corps.
- **Have a disability:** Pre-employment transition services.
- **Have a record:** Reentry programs and employers who hire people with records.

Then compare youth employability services using these questions:

- Is it free?
- Which employers hire graduates?
- What support continues after placement?
- How do you measure results?

Red flags include upfront fees, guaranteed placement, and pressure to sign quickly. Legitimate youth employability services rarely charge eligible participants.

## How to Find Youth Employability Services Near You

Start with your local American Job Center. Then work down this list:

1. Search the American Job Center Finder on CareerOneStop with your ZIP code.
1. Ask for the WIOA Youth coordinator.
1. Check your city or county website for summer and year-round programs.
1. Ask your school counselor or library about nonprofits.
1. Call 211 for local referrals.

## Tips for Parents and Caregivers

Parents shape how young people approach work. Small actions help:

- Gather documents early: photo ID, birth certificate, and Social Security card.
- Check work permit rules. Requirements for minors vary by state.
- Practice interviews at home.
- Talk through a first-paycheck budget.
- Research youth employability services together, so your teen owns the decisions.

Help with rides and schedules. Don't run the job search for them.

## Are Youth Employability Services Free?

Most publicly funded youth employability services are free for eligible participants. Some nonprofits use sliding-scale fees. Confirm costs before enrolling.

## What Age Can I Start?

Many youth employability services start at 14. Job Corps and YouthBuild start at 16. Most end at 24.

## Do I Need to Be in School?

No. Some youth employability services serve in-school youth, and others serve out-of-school youth. Federal youth funding prioritizes out-of-school youth.

## What If I Have Never Had a Job?

That is a normal starting point. Programs help you build a first resume from school, volunteering, and family responsibilities. Paid work experience gives you a real reference.

## How Long Do Youth Employability Services Last?

It varies. A workshop can take days. Many summer programs run up to 12 weeks. Job Corps students can train for up to three years. Apprenticeships often run longer.
